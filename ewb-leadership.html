My role as
President

Taking on the presidency of Engineers Without Borders at KLS GIT meant more than a title. It was a full commitment to organizing, motivating, and executing — from planning event themes to managing a diverse team of designers, writers, coordinators, and photographers.

Every event started with identifying a real-world problem students needed to engage with. I worked closely with faculty advisors, coordinated with team leads, made on-ground decisions, and ensured each event ran smoothly from kick-off to wrap-up.

Beyond execution, I focused on building a chapter culture of ownership — where every member felt responsible for outcomes. That meant weekly syncs, design reviews, logistics management, and post-event feedback loops.

01
🌾 Field + Tech
AgriVision — Smart Farming for the Future
📅 25 July 2024 📍 Local Farm & KLS GIT Campus
Led by Shreejit
40+
Students Participated

About the Event

Took students to nearby farms to experience agricultural challenges first-hand. Farmers explained real issues around irrigation, soil health, and crop yield. Students then designed technical posters proposing innovative engineering solutions — IoT-based irrigation, solar pumps, waste management, and low-cost machinery.

Key Highlights

  • Organized farm field visits and farmer interaction sessions end-to-end
  • Briefed students on problem framing before the poster design activity
  • Coordinated faculty evaluation panel for poster assessment
  • Bridged the gap between rural agricultural needs and student innovation

Outcome

Students gained practical insights into farming problems. Farmers appreciated the involvement, strengthening the technology-community link. Encouraged application of engineering to solve real agricultural issues.

🏆 Inter-College · Flagship
AgriVision 2.0 — Inter-College Innovation Challenge
📅 30–31 January 2026 📍 KLS GIT Campus ⏱ 2-Day Event
Led by Shreejit
83+
Teams · Inter-College

About the Event — Flagship

The biggest event under my tenure. AgriVision 2.0 was a 2-day inter-college prototype competition where teams designed and built working solutions for agricultural challenges. Day 1 focused on development with mentor guidance. Day 2 saw the Top 15 teams pitching their prototypes to an expert jury panel.

Key Highlights

  • Planned and executed a 2-day inter-college event from scratch
  • Managed 83+ competing teams across multiple colleges
  • Coordinated judges, mentors, venue, and logistics simultaneously
  • Led inauguration, briefing, and shortlisting of Top 15 teams
  • Ensured smooth prototype demonstration and judging process

Outcome — Largest event under EWB KLS GIT

Successfully hosted 83+ teams. Elevated the chapter's visibility at inter-college level. Prototype ideas were evaluated on innovation, feasibility, and real-world impact — inspiring young engineers to build for agriculture.
